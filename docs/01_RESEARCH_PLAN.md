## 3. Research Opportunities (explored, then narrowed)

I considered twelve directions before selecting three. Recording the rejected ones matters — an examiner may ask "why not deep learning?"

### Considered and **selected**

| Direction | Why it survived |
|---|---|
| **Physics prior as a robustness mechanism** | The hybrid already exists. The *interesting* question is not "is it more accurate?" (published many times) but **"does the physics prior make it degrade more gracefully out-of-distribution?"** That question is open, cheap to answer, and the answer is genuinely unknown in advance. |
| **Conditional calibration of prediction intervals** | Split conformal is implemented; extending to **Mondrian (group-conditional) conformal and CQR** is ~100 lines and produces a real finding about *where* the band lies. Directly serves range anxiety: a band that fails exactly in cold weather is worse than useless. |
| **SoH→range error propagation** | Closes the project's own title. Converts `soh` from a slider into an estimated quantity with a measured error distribution, then asks **how many km of range error one percentage point of SoH error actually costs.** Pure EV engineering; no new ML needed. |

### Considered and **rejected** (with reasons — useful in the viva)

| Direction | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| LSTM/GRU for sequence-level range | The target is a **trip-level scalar** (Wh/km), not a sequence. An LSTM over 1 Hz traces would need per-second targets we do not have, and with 3 BEVs would overfit badly. *Optionally* justified for SoH from charging curves only. |
| Transformers / foundation models | No dataset here is remotely large enough. Pure buzzword risk. |
| Reinforcement learning for eco-routing | Different problem; needs a simulator and a reward model. Scope explosion. |
| Federated learning | Published on VED, but we have 3 vehicles. Meaningless at this scale. |
| Battery anomaly / knee detection | Interesting and real, but a second full project. **Avoid.** |
| Electrochemical (P2D/SPM) battery modelling | Requires cell parameters we do not have. Would take the whole year. |
| Hardware logger (ESP32 / BMS UART) | **Excluded by the synopsis itself** ("fully software-based, no additional hardware") and by `CLAUDE.md` rule 8. |
| Live traffic APIs | No free reproducible historical tier (investigation §6.3). Replaced by a derived congestion proxy. |
| Charging-station / route-planning optimization | Scope explosion; not in the synopsis. |

### 4.2 Legitimate combination rules (enforce these in code)

1. **VED + eVED + ERA5 = one table.** ✅ Legitimate: eVED enriches identical VED records; ERA5 joins on a genuine physical (place, time) key.
   *Stated caveat:* ERA5 is a ~9–25 km reanalysis grid — an area average, not the microclimate at the wheel.
2. **Battery cell data is NEVER merged with trip data.** ❌ Different physical objects, conditions and sampling. Shared column *names* are not a join key.
3. **The two halves couple only through the physical equation**
   `range = usable_energy(SoH, SoC, T) / consumption(route, weather, state)`
   Each side fitted on its own real data; the coupling is a **propagation study**, tagged `COUPLED-SIM` in every table, figure caption and UI panel.
4. **Domains are never pooled.** VED, ZTBus and e-scooter data are separate *domains* in a transfer experiment — fit on one, re-identify vehicle parameters, evaluate on another. Pooling them would be the experiment's failure mode, not its method.

**Implementation:** every DataFrame produced by a loader carries a `provenance` column ∈ {`REAL`,`SYNTHETIC`,`COUPLED-SIM`} and a `source_dataset` column. `src/evaluation/` **refuses to emit a metrics table mixing provenance tags** unless explicitly forced. This makes rule 3 mechanically unbreakable — a strong thing to show an examiner.

### 4.3 The role of synthetic data after the rewrite

Synthetic data stays, but **must be rebuilt to remove the circularity** (investigation §3), and is restricted to two uses:

- **(i) Testing & CI.** Fast deterministic fixtures so `pytest` stays ~40 s and the app runs before data is downloaded. No results reported.
- **(ii) Controlled identifiability experiments.** Questions that *require* known ground truth, e.g. *"given N noisy trips, can we recover Crr and drivetrain efficiency, and how does the error scale with N?"* Real data can never answer this, because the true Crr is unknown.

**The de-circularisation requirement (non-negotiable):** the synthetic *truth* must be generated by a **higher-fidelity simulator than the baseline** — a per-second speed trace with acceleration dynamics, a motor/inverter efficiency **map** (η varying with torque and speed, not a constant 0.80), temperature-dependent pack internal resistance, and SoC-dependent pack voltage. The trip-level road-load baseline then becomes a genuinely *simplified* model of it. Only then is "can ML correct the physics?" a real question rather than an algebraic identity.

**Claims permitted from synthetic experiments:** *"the estimator recovers Crr to ±X % from N trips"*, *"the pipeline is numerically correct"*. **Claims forbidden:** any statement of real-world accuracy, and any accuracy number in the abstract or conclusion.

## 5. Proposed Research Questions

Three questions. Each has an outcome that is **genuinely unknown before the experiment runs** — that is the test of a real research question.

> ### RQ1 — Does a physics prior buy *robustness*, or only accuracy?
> *When an EV consumption model is evaluated under progressively harder distribution shift (unseen routes → unseen season/temperature → unseen vehicle class), does the physics-informed hybrid degrade more slowly than a pure-ML model of equal capacity?*
>
> **Why it is not trivial:** published hybrid papers report in-distribution accuracy. The prior's *real* selling point — that road-load physics extrapolates where trees cannot — is asserted far more often than it is measured.
> **Headline metric:** **degradation ratio** `D = MAPE_out-of-distribution / MAPE_in-distribution`. A model can win on accuracy and lose on D.
> **Either outcome is publishable-quality reporting:** if the hybrid holds up, the prior is justified; if it does not, that is a genuinely useful negative result about physics-informed ML.

> ### RQ2 — Is the range band trustworthy *where it matters*?
> *Do split-conformal intervals that achieve correct marginal coverage retain coverage conditionally — within cold-temperature, steep-grade, and high-congestion subgroups — and does Mondrian (group-conditional) conformal or CQR repair it, at what cost in interval width?*
>
> **Why it matters physically:** a rider is stranded by a band that fails on a cold hilly evening, not by one that fails on average. Marginal coverage is the wrong guarantee for range anxiety.
> **Metrics:** marginal coverage, **worst-subgroup coverage**, mean relative width, and the width/coverage trade-off curve.
> **Bonus:** this also diagnoses the existing 0.73-vs-0.90 quantile defect (B4).

> ### RQ3 — How much of the range error is actually the *battery's* fault?
> *When SoH is estimated (with its measured leave-one-battery-out error distribution) instead of assumed, how does the range prediction interval widen — and what fraction of total range-error variance is attributable to consumption-model error vs. SoH-estimation error vs. temperature-derating uncertainty, as a function of trip length, SoC and vehicle age?*
>
> **Why it closes the project:** it turns the word "SoH-coupled" in the title from an assertion into a measurement, and produces an engineering deliverable with immediate practical value: **"km of range error per percentage point of SoH error."**
> **Honesty requirement:** tagged `COUPLED-SIM` everywhere, because no public dataset provides trips and pack SoH for the same vehicle. This limitation is stated in the abstract, not buried.

**Optional RQ4 (Enhancement tier only):** *Does SoH estimated from real vehicle packs (EVBattery) behave differently from lab-cell SoH (NASA/Oxford) when propagated into range?* — directly attacks the project's own stated limitation. Attempt only if Phases 0–4 finish early.

## 6. Proposed Experiments

Each experiment = one file in `src/experiments/`, one config, one output directory `reports/<exp_id>/`, **5 seeds**, mean ± std reported.

### E0 — Leakage demonstration (the control experiment) · *Core*
Train and evaluate the same model under **random row-wise** splitting and under **route-wise** splitting, on the same real data.
**Purpose:** quantify, in your own numbers, how much accuracy a naive split fabricates. Expect the random split to look dramatically (and falsely) better.
**Why it is worth a whole experiment:** it is the single most persuasive slide in the viva, it proves you understood leakage rather than merely avoided it, and it pre-empts the "how did you prevent data leakage?" question with evidence instead of a claim.
**Output:** `reports/E0_leakage/` + one bar chart.

### E1 — Model comparison under route-wise splits (real data) · *Core*
Re-run the existing six models (`rated_range`, `physics_only`, `linear`, `random_forest`, `gbm`, `hybrid_physics_ml`) on **real VED/eVED+ERA5 trips**, route-wise grouped, 5 seeds.
**Add:** XGBoost/LightGBM-class GBM as a stronger pure-ML contender so the hybrid's win is not an artefact of a weak opponent; and a **physics-with-identified-parameters** baseline (post-calibration) — a much fairer physics baseline than nominal constants.
**Purpose:** the first honest accuracy table the project will ever have. Replaces every number currently in `reports/`.

### E2 — Distribution-shift ladder (**RQ1**) · *Research*
The same model set evaluated under four progressively harder protocols:

| Level | Split | Question answered |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | Random rows | (leaky control, from E0) |
| L1 | Unseen **routes** | Current standard |
| L2 | Unseen **season / temperature band** — train on warm months, test on cold | Does it survive winter it never saw? |
| L3 | Unseen **vehicle** — leave-one-Leaf-out within VED | Does it survive a different car of the same model? |
| L4 | Unseen **vehicle class** — fit on VED cars, re-identify parameters, test on **ZTBus buses** and **e-scooter** trips | Does the *model form* transfer when only parameters change? |

**Primary metric:** degradation ratio `D = MAPE(Lk)/MAPE(L1)` per model.
**Hypothesis (pre-registered, to be written down *before* running):** `D_hybrid < D_pure-ML` at L2 and L4, even if the hybrid's L1 advantage is small.
**This is the project's headline experiment.**

### E3 — Feature-group ablation on real data · *Core*
Re-run the existing ablation (`route` / `weather` / `state` groups) on real data, **plus a new `traffic-proxy` group** (speed deficit, stop rate, speed σ).
**Why it now means something:** on synthetic data the answer was guaranteed by the generator. On real data it is unknown — and it is the evidence for **synopsis objective 3** (impact of weather, traffic, driving behaviour).
**Report:** ΔMAPE per group, with 5-seed error bars, for both the hybrid and the pure-ML model. Report the two separately — the hybrid is expected to be *less* sensitive to losing route features because the physics layer still consumes them internally (this asymmetry is already noted in `pipeline.py:49-50` and should be stated, not hidden).

### E4 — Interval calibration under shift (**RQ2**) · *Research*
Compare four interval methods: (a) split conformal (existing), (b) conformalized quantile regression (CQR — the principled fix for B4), (c) **Mondrian conformal** with groups = temperature bin × route type, (d) naive quantile regression (existing, as the negative control).
**Report for each:** marginal coverage, **per-subgroup coverage table**, worst-subgroup coverage, mean relative width, and a coverage-vs-width trade-off plot.
**Then repeat under the L2 shift** — this is where marginal coverage is expected to survive while conditional coverage collapses.

### E5 — SoH estimation: LOBO + cross-dataset transfer · *Core→Research*
Keep the existing LOBO protocol; run it on **real** NASA cells with real health indicators (IR proxy, CC-charge time, time in voltage window, IC-curve peak, temperature rise).
**Add the transfer test:** train on NASA, test on **Oxford** (different chemistry, format and duty cycle) without retraining.
**Enforce rule 2 of `CLAUDE.md`:** assert in code that no feature derives from the cycle's own capacity. Add an explicit **leakage unit test** that fails if a capacity-derived column enters `SOH_FEATURES`.
**Expect real LOBO MAE of 2–5 percentage points**, i.e. several times worse than the current synthetic 1.11 pp. Reporting that honestly is a strength.

### E6 — SoH→range propagation and variance decomposition (**RQ3**) · *Research*
1. Take the E5 out-of-fold SoH error distribution (real, per-cell).
2. Replace oracle `soh` in the range pipeline with `soh_estimated = soh_true + ε`, ε sampled from that real distribution.
3. Propagate through `usable_energy_wh` → range → interval, by Monte Carlo.
4. **Decompose total range-error variance** into: consumption-model error, SoH-estimation error, temperature-derate uncertainty.
5. Produce the engineering deliverable: **km of range error per 1 pp of SoH error**, plotted against trip length, starting SoC, and vehicle age.
**Tag everything `COUPLED-SIM`.**

### E7 — Physics-parameter identification · *Core*
Fit `crr` and `drivetrain_eff` (and, where identifiable, `Cd·A`) by constrained least squares on: (a) Argonne D3 dynamometer runs (zero grade, zero wind — the cleanest possible setting), (b) flat real VED trips.
**Report:** fitted values with confidence intervals, and the resulting improvement of the *physics-only* baseline. Also run the synthetic identifiability study (§4.3-ii) to show how the estimate converges with N.
**This is what makes `src/config.py` defensible instead of "PLACEHOLDER".**

### E8 — Degradation-constant calibration · *Enhancement*
Fit the six invented constants in `project_soh` (fade per EFC, Arrhenius temperature factor, DoD exponent, charge-cap factor) against NASA + Oxford fade trajectories.
**Report goodness of fit and confidence intervals**, and keep the "indicative, lab-cell-derived, not a vehicle pack" caption in the UI. If the fit is poor, **say so and widen the caption** — do not force it.

### E9 — Explainability: SHAP vs. counterfactual agreement · *Research*
Compute SHAP values on the hybrid's ML residual layer; compare the ranking against the existing counterfactual attribution in `advisor.explain()`.
**The interesting question:** do a *local* additive attribution (SHAP, on the residual only) and a *physical* counterfactual (re-running the full physics+ML pipeline with the factor neutralised) agree on which factor cost the most km? **Where they disagree is scientifically informative** — it localises what the physics layer explains versus what the ML layer absorbs.
**Deliverable:** agreement rate (e.g. Spearman ρ of factor rankings) + the disagreement case study.

### E10 — Error analysis · *Core*
Residuals vs. temperature, grade, distance, speed, SoC, congestion proxy. Identify the worst-predicted trip deciles and characterise them.
**Every good paper has this section and most student projects skip it.** It is also the cheapest source of real insight.

## 7. ML Strategy

### 7.1 Models — and the justification for each

The rule: **every model must earn its place by answering a question, not by sounding advanced.**

| Model | Role | Why it fits this data | Limitation | Compared against | B.Tech-feasible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ConstantRated** *[KEEP]* | Industry floor | This is literally what a dashboard "guess-o-meter" does | Ignores everything | — | ✅ trivial |
| **PhysicsOnly (nominal)** *[KEEP]* | Domain floor | Pure first-principles, zero learning | Wrong parameters | ConstantRated | ✅ exists |
| **PhysicsOnly (identified)** *[NEW, E7]* | **Fair** domain baseline | Same model, parameters fitted from data | Still no residual learning | PhysicsOnly nominal | ✅ least squares |
| **Linear / Ridge** *[KEEP]* | Interpretable statistical baseline | Coefficients are directly readable in physical units; extrapolates linearly | Cannot capture interactions | Physics | ✅ exists |
| **Random Forest** *[KEEP]* | Synopsis's named model | Handles non-linearity, no scaling needed | **Cannot extrapolate** beyond training envelope — which is exactly why it scored 18.6 % (worse than physics at 11.3 %). **This is a finding, not a bug.** | GBM | ✅ exists |
| **Gradient Boosting (HGB / LightGBM)** *[EXTEND]* | Strong pure-ML contender | Best-in-class for small-to-medium tabular data | Same extrapolation failure | Hybrid | ✅ exists |
| **HybridResidual** *[KEEP]* | **The proposal** | Physics carries the extrapolation; ML corrects the residual. Log-ratio keeps it positive and scale-free | Inherits physics-model error if the prior is wrong | All of the above | ✅ exists |
| **CQR / Mondrian conformal** *[NEW, E4]* | Calibrated intervals | Distribution-free, finite-sample guarantee, assumption-light | Guarantees marginal coverage only unless grouped | Split conformal | ✅ ~100 lines |
| **GRU/1-D CNN on charging curves** *[OPTIONAL, E5]* | SoH only | *Here* the input genuinely is a sequence (voltage/current curve) | Needs ≫8 cells; overfits on NASA | RF/GBM on hand-crafted HIs | ⚠️ only with Severson's 124 cells |

**Explicitly not used, and why (viva-ready answers):**
- *"Why no LSTM for range?"* — The target is a trip-level scalar. Sequence models need per-timestep targets we do not have, and with 3 BEVs an LSTM would memorise vehicles. We use a sequence model **only** where the input is truly sequential (charging curves, E5-optional).
- *"Why no deep learning at all?"* — On tabular data of this size, gradient-boosted trees are the stronger and better-understood choice; adding a neural net would add parameters, not evidence.

### 7.2 Evaluation methodology

- **Metrics:** MAE and RMSE (Wh/km), MAPE (%), and — replacing the redundant `range_MAPE` (investigation §3) — **`range_km_error`**, the absolute error in predicted kilometres at a stated SoC and SoH. That is the quantity a rider cares about, and unlike `range_MAPE` it does not algebraically cancel.
- **Protocol:** grouped splits at every level (`route_id`, `vehicle_id`, `battery_id`, season). `GroupKFold` for CV; a held-out test set touched **once**, at the end.
- **Seeds:** 5 per experiment; report **mean ± std**. Single-seed results are not reported.
- **Statistical care:** with only 3 BEVs, differences between models on vehicle-wise splits must be reported with paired per-route errors and a paired test (e.g. Wilcoxon signed-rank), **not** as a bare comparison of two means. State the sample size next to every claim.
- **No hyper-parameter tuning on the test set.** Tuning happens inside the CV loop on the development set only.

## 8. EV / Battery Analysis — connecting the AI to real vehicle behaviour

This section answers *"is this an EV project or just an ML project?"*

### 8.1 Relationships currently modelled, and their status

| Relationship | Physics | Currently | After the plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mass → rolling + grade force | `F = m·g·(Crr·cosθ + sinθ)` | ✅ implemented | ✅ + **real varying mass from ZTBus passenger counts** |
| Speed → aerodynamic drag | `F ∝ ½ρ·Cd·A·v²` | ✅ implemented | ✅ + Cd·A identified (E7) |
| Air temperature → air density | `ρ = P/(R·T)` | ✅ implemented | ✅ |
| Grade → potential energy | `F = m·g·sinθ` | ✅ implemented | ✅ + real eVED elevation |
| Stops → kinetic-energy loss | `½mv²·(1/η − η_regen)` | ✅ implemented | ✅ + stops derived from 1 Hz traces |
| Aux load → energy over time | `P_aux · t` | ✅ implemented | ✅ where VED provides the channel |
| **SoH → usable energy** | `E = E_nom·SoH·derate(T)·SoC` | ⚠️ SoH is an oracle slider | ✅ **estimated, with propagated uncertainty (E6)** |
| **Temperature → usable capacity** | derating | ❌ invented constant (−0.6 %/°C) | ✅ **fitted from NASA/Oxford temperature-varied cells (E8)** |

### 8.2 Relationships to **add** (each justified by available data)

| New relationship | Physical basis | Data that supports it |
|---|---|---|
| **SoC → pack voltage → I²R loss** | At fixed power, lower pack voltage ⇒ higher current ⇒ losses scale with I². Consumption should rise measurably at low SoC. | VED logs SoC, voltage **and** current at 1 Hz — this is directly observable, not assumed. **Also finally gives the dead `soc_start` feature (B7) a physical job.** |
| **Temperature → internal resistance → efficiency** | Cold cells have higher R; more energy is lost as heat. Distinct from the capacity derate. | VED trips across a full Michigan year (−20 °C to +35 °C) + ERA5. |
| **Regenerative braking effectiveness vs. SoC and temperature** | Regen is limited at high SoC and in the cold (charge acceptance). The code assumes a constant `regen_eff = 0.40`. | VED: negative battery current episodes on descents, stratified by SoC and temperature. **A genuinely interesting sub-finding.** |
| **Driving aggressiveness → consumption** | Positive kinetic energy / accel variance is a classic eco-driving metric. | Derived from 1 Hz speed traces — replaces the synthetic 3-level `style` integer with a *measured* continuous index. |
| **Congestion → stop-go energy** | Stops dominate urban consumption. | `speed_limit − observed speed` from eVED. **This is how synopsis objective 3 (traffic) is actually delivered.** |

### 8.3 Relationships deliberately **not** claimed

- Cell-level degradation mechanisms (SEI growth, lithium plating) — no data, and no measurement that could distinguish them here.
- Thermal-management / HVAC energy split — confounded with ambient temperature in VED; cannot be separated honestly.
- Calendar vs. cycle ageing decomposition — requires storage-test data with controlled rest periods; not available for our cells at the needed resolution.

## 9. Explainability / Interpretability

The system explains itself at **three levels** — and this layered structure is itself a defensible design choice.

| Level | Mechanism | Question it answers | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Structural** | The hybrid's decomposition `prediction = physics × exp(residual)` | *"How much of this prediction is first-principles physics and how much is learned correction?"* — a single, directly displayable ratio | ✅ exists, **surface it in the UI** |
| **2. Physical / counterfactual** | `advisor.explain()` re-runs the whole pipeline with one factor neutralised | *"How many km would I regain without this headwind?"* — an actionable, units-correct answer | ✅ exists *[KEEP]* |
| **3. Statistical / local** | **SHAP on the ML residual layer** *[NEW, E9]* | *"Which features drive the correction the physics could not explain?"* | ❌ to add |

**Why keep counterfactuals when SHAP exists** (a likely viva question): SHAP explains the *residual model* only — it is blind to everything the physics layer already accounts for, and its units are log-ratio, not kilometres. The counterfactual runs the *entire* system and returns kilometres, which is what a rider can act on. They answer different questions, and **E9's comparison of the two is itself a small research contribution.**

**Global interpretability:** permutation importance (grouped by feature family, to respect correlations between e.g. grade and elevation gain), plus partial-dependence / ICE curves for the physically meaningful variables (temperature, grade, speed, SoC). These should be **checked against physics**: if the PDP for temperature has the wrong sign, that is a bug, not a discovery. Add this as an automated sanity check.

## 10. Validation Strategy

### 10.1 Leakage register (every risk, its mechanism, and its control)

| Risk | Mechanism in *this* project | Control | Enforced by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Route leakage | Same route in train and test ⇒ memorised geometry | `GroupKFold`/`GroupShuffleSplit` on `route_id` | ✅ exists + `assert` (`pipeline.py:77`) |
| Vehicle leakage | Same car in train and test ⇒ memorised vehicle | Leave-one-vehicle-out (E2-L3) | **[NEW]** |
| **Battery-label leakage** | Using a cycle's own capacity (or any derivative) as a feature | Feature whitelist + **a unit test that fails if a capacity-derived column enters `SOH_FEATURES`** | `CLAUDE.md` rule 2, **[NEW] test** |
| **Temporal leakage** | Training on later cycles/months and testing on earlier ones | Time-ordered splits for any temporal claim; season split is **train-warm → test-cold**, never shuffled | **[NEW]** |
| **Calibration leakage** | Conformal calibration set sharing routes with train **or** test | Three-way route-disjoint split | ✅ exists — **keep and re-assert after refactor** |
| **Preprocessing leakage** | Scalers/imputers fitted on the full dataset before splitting | All preprocessing inside `sklearn` `Pipeline` objects fitted per fold | **[FIX]** — currently `make_pipeline` is used for `linear` only |
| **Weather leakage** | Using a *forecast* issued after the trip, or a same-day observation the driver could not have had | Use ERA5 reanalysis, and state clearly that this represents *"weather as it turned out"*, not *"the forecast available at departure"*. For the deployment story, note that a real user would have forecast uncertainty on top. **Stating this honestly pre-empts a sharp examiner question.** | **[NEW]** |
| **Tuning leakage** | Selecting hyper-parameters using test performance | Nested CV on dev only; test touched once | **[NEW] protocol** |
| Selection bias | Silently dropping hard trips during cleaning | **Log every exclusion with a reason and a count**; publish the attrition table | **[NEW]** |

### 10.2 Generalization ladder
Exactly the L0→L4 ladder of E2. **Claims are permitted only at the level actually tested**, and every results table states its level.

### 10.3 Uncertainty
Report marginal **and** per-subgroup coverage (E4), always with interval width — coverage without width is meaningless (a band of ±∞ covers everything).

### 10.4 Robustness
Feature-noise injection (GPS jitter on elevation, ±2 °C on temperature, ±1 m/s on wind) → measure MAPE and coverage degradation. This directly simulates what happens with a real 90 m-resolution SRTM elevation or a coarse weather grid, and it is cheap.

### 10.5 Claims discipline — the sentence to keep on the wall

| ✅ Permitted | ❌ Forbidden |
|---|---|
| "On 3 BEVs in VED, route-wise, hybrid achieves X % MAPE (5 seeds)" | "Our model achieves X % accuracy for electric vehicles" |
| "Cold-weather coverage drops to Y %, motivating Mondrian conformal" | "Our intervals are 90 % reliable" |
| "SoH error of 1 pp costs ≈ Z km at 80 % SoC" (`COUPLED-SIM`) | "Our system predicts range from battery health" |
| "The physics prior reduced the degradation ratio from A to B" | "Physics-informed ML is more robust" (unqualified) |
| "Synthetic study: Crr recovered to ±X % from N trips" | Any synthetic number quoted as real-world accuracy |

## 13. Research Contribution — stated honestly

### 13.1 What is **not** novel (say this before an examiner does)
- Physics-informed / hybrid road-load + ML residual for EV consumption — **published** (multiple 2023–2026 works).
- Conformal prediction and CQR for EV energy/range — **published** (e.g. *Energy Informatics* 2026 comparing SVR-CP, adaptive CP, LightGBM-CQR).
- SHAP for EV energy feature importance — **published** (WEVJ 2021 onward).
- SoH-coupled range estimation with uncertainty — **exists**, mainly in the filtering/control literature.

**We claim none of these as our invention.** Claiming them would be the fastest way to lose credibility.

### 13.2 What is genuinely under-served, and what we contribute

> **Contribution 1 (primary) — A distribution-shift benchmark for physics-informed EV consumption models on open data.**
> A reproducible four-level shift ladder (route → season → vehicle → **vehicle class**) evaluating whether the physics prior buys robustness rather than accuracy, using the **degradation ratio** as the headline metric. Cross-vehicle-class transfer (car → bus → e-scooter) with parameter re-identification is, to our knowledge, not systematically reported on public data. **Either outcome is a useful result.**

> **Contribution 2 — Conditional, not marginal, calibration of EV range intervals.**
> A subgroup-coverage analysis (temperature × route type) showing where marginal coverage hides conditional failure, with Mondrian conformal as the remedy and an explicit width/coverage trade-off. Practically motivated: range anxiety is a *conditional* problem.

> **Contribution 3 — A range-error budget attributable to SoH estimation.**
> Propagating a *measured* LOBO SoH error distribution into range and decomposing the variance, yielding the engineering quantity **"km of range error per percentage point of SoH error"** as a function of trip length and SoC. Clearly labelled `COUPLED-SIM`.

> **Contribution 4 (engineering) — A reproducible, leakage-audited open pipeline** combining VED/eVED + ERA5 + NASA/Oxford with enforced provenance tagging and an executable leakage-check suite.

### 13.3 Engineering vs. research — the line

| Engineering implementation (necessary, not a contribution) | Research contribution (a result that could be wrong) |
|---|---|
| Building loaders, feature pipeline, Streamlit app | The shift-ladder finding (RQ1) |
| Running SHAP, running conformal | The conditional-coverage finding (RQ2) |
| Fitting a model, producing a MAPE table | The SoH→range error budget (RQ3) |
| Reproducibility tooling | The SHAP-vs-counterfactual disagreement analysis (E9) |

### 13.4 What a **publishable paper** would additionally need
Be realistic with the guide about this:
- **More vehicles.** 3 BEVs is the binding limitation. A workshop/conference paper could go out on VED+ZTBus+e-scooter; a journal would want more BEVs (EVBattery or a fleet partner).
- **Statistical rigour at scale** — the shift-ladder claim needs more than 3 vehicles to be more than suggestive.
- **A real end-to-end SoH validation** — trips *and* pack SoH from the same vehicles. Not publicly available today; this is the honest ceiling.
- **Comparison against at least two published baselines reimplemented faithfully**, not just internal baselines.

**Realistic target:** a well-executed **national/international conference paper or a student-track submission** on Contributions 1–2. **Do not promise a journal paper to anyone.**

### 15.2 Limitations to state in the report and the viva (unprompted)

1. **Three BEVs.** Vehicle-wise conclusions rest on 3 Nissan Leafs of one model year in one city. This is the single biggest limitation. *Say it first.*
2. **No end-to-end SoH validation exists.** No public dataset has both a vehicle's trips and its pack SoH trajectory. RQ3 is therefore a **propagation study** (`COUPLED-SIM`), not an empirical validation.
3. **Lab cells ≠ vehicle packs.** NASA/Oxford cells are 2 Ah 18650s and 740 mAh pouches; a pack adds cell imbalance, BMS behaviour, and thermal gradients. (EVBattery partially addresses this — Enhancement tier.)
4. **ERA5 is a ~9–25 km grid average**, not the weather at the wheel. Microclimate, local gusts and road-surface wetness are unresolved.
5. **"Weather as it turned out", not "the forecast at departure."** A deployed system would carry forecast uncertainty on top of model uncertainty. We do not model that.
6. **Traffic is a proxy**, derived from observed speed vs. speed limit — not a measured traffic feed.
7. **Driving style is derived**, not labelled. An aggressiveness index from acceleration is a proxy for driver intent.
8. **Payload is unobserved in VED** — a known confounder. Only ZTBus provides real mass variation.
9. **Geography.** Ann Arbor, Michigan and Zurich. **No Indian road, traffic or climate data** underpins the primary results — relevant given the project's context and the e-scooter target vehicle.
10. **Elevation resolution** (SRTM ~90 m) smooths short steep features.
11. **Degradation projections remain indicative**, fitted on lab cells, with the uncertainty shown.
12. **Conformal guarantees assume exchangeability**, which distribution shift violates by construction — which is precisely why RQ2 measures coverage *under* shift rather than assuming it.

## 18. Final Expected System

### 18.1 What the finished project will do

**A rider-facing advisor that:**
- Takes a real origin→destination route (OSRM) with real elevation and real current weather, or a cached preset offline.
- Predicts consumption (Wh/km) with a **physics-informed hybrid calibrated on real BEV data**, and converts it to range using usable energy derived from SoC, an **estimated** SoH, and a **fitted** temperature derate.
- Reports a **90 % prediction interval whose conditional coverage has been measured**, not just assumed — including in the cold and on hills.
- Explains the prediction three ways: the physics-vs-ML split, counterfactual "you would gain N km if…", and SHAP on the residual.
- Gives calibrated, uncertainty-aware battery advice, with indicative degradation projections whose constants were fitted (and whose limitations are printed on screen).

**A research artefact that can state, with evidence:**
- How much accuracy a naive random split fabricates (**E0**) — the leakage question, answered with numbers.
- How every model degrades across four levels of distribution shift, and whether the physics prior slows that degradation (**E2 / RQ1**).
- Where the range band's coverage fails conditionally, and what repairs it at what cost in width (**E4 / RQ2**).
- How many km of range error one percentage point of SoH error costs, and what fraction of the total error budget the battery contributes (**E6 / RQ3**).
- Which factors actually drive consumption in real data — weather, congestion proxy, driving aggressiveness (**E3**, delivering synopsis objective 3).
- What the vehicle's real Crr and drivetrain efficiency are, with confidence intervals (**E7**).

**A reproducible repository:** clean clone → `scripts/fetch_data.py` → `scripts/run_all_experiments.py` → every table and figure in the report regenerates, with seeds, data hashes and git revisions recorded in each experiment's manifest.

### 18.2 Viva question bank — and where each answer will come from

| Examiner question | Answer, and its evidence |
|---|---|
| *Why this dataset?* | VED is the only open dataset with 1 Hz **HV battery current and voltage** plus GPS, so measured Wh/km is computable rather than inferred. Its weakness — 3 BEVs — is why ZTBus and the e-scooter set were added as separate domains. **DATA_CARD.md** |
| *Why this model?* | Six models compared on real data under identical splits; the hybrid is justified by the shift ladder, not by in-distribution MAPE alone. **E1, E2** |
| *Why is this feature important?* | Ablation with 5-seed error bars, plus SHAP, plus physics-checked partial-dependence curves. **E3, E9** |
| *How do you know it generalizes?* | We do not claim it in general. We report exactly four tested levels and the degradation at each. **E2** |
| *How does temperature affect prediction?* | Three separate physical channels — air density (physics), capacity derate (fitted, E8), and internal resistance/efficiency (learned residual) — plus a live sweep in the UI. **Tab 4** |
| *How does degradation affect range?* | The km-per-pp-of-SoH curve, versus trip length and SoC. **E6** |
| *Why better than a baseline?* | Four baselines including a **fairly calibrated** physics model, not a straw man. **E1, E7** |
| *How did you prevent data leakage?* | A leakage register (§10.1), grouped splits, executable leakage tests — **and E0, which quantifies what leakage would have bought us.** |
| *What happens when conditions change?* | That is RQ1 and RQ2, and both have subgroup tables. **E2, E4** |
| *Can it explain itself?* | Three levels, and we measured where two of them disagree. **E9** |
| *What is new?* | Contributions 1–3 (§13.2), stated alongside an explicit list of what is **not** new (§13.1). |
| *What are the limitations?* | Twelve, listed in §15.2, stated before being asked. |
| *Is your synthetic result real?* | No — and §4.3 explains exactly what synthetic data is and is not used for. The old circular numbers are archived, labelled, and discussed as a lesson. |

### 18.3 What the project will **not** be able to do
Predict range for an arbitrary EV it has never seen without re-identifying vehicle parameters · validate SoH end-to-end on a real vehicle's trips · use live traffic · replace a BMS · make claims about Indian roads or climate from this data · guarantee interval coverage under arbitrary distribution shift.

**Knowing and stating these is part of the deliverable.**
